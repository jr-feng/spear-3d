SPEAR-3D: From Streaming Reconstruction to Explicit Reasoning—A
Neuro-Symbolic Approach for 3D Visual Grounding
First A. Author,Member, IEEE,Second B. Author, and Third C. Author
Abstract—Open-vocabulary 3D scene instance reconstruction
is a crucial capability for autonomous robots working in complex
environments. It requires both efficient streaming 3D instance con-
struction and accurate language-driven object localization within
the constructed scene. Existing methods face two major challenges.
First, online systems often divide 2D perception, semantic feature
extraction, and 3D reconstruction into separate modules, leading
to high latency and inconsistent scene representations. Second,
directly using Large Language Models (LLMs) to spatial reasoning
tasks may lead to spatial hallucinations, as the reasoning process
lacks explicit constraints from reconstructed 3D geometry. To
address these issues, we propose SPEAR-3D, a framework that
combines prompt-conditioned Open-vocabulary streaming 3D
scene instance reconstruction with explicit neuro-symbolic spatial
reasoning. Our reconstruction module incrementally constructs an
instance-aware 3D scene from RGB-D streams,and this streaming
reconstruction stage operates at 10 FPS on an RTX 3090. Based
on the reconstructed scene, we introduce a Neuro-Symbolic Query
Graph Grounding (NS-Grounding) module that parses natural-
language instructions into structured query graphs and matches
them against a geometry-derived 3D scene graph. Experimental
results show that SPEAR-3D achieves 10 FPS in streaming
reconstruction, reaches 60.4 AP25 on ScanNet200, and improves
grounding accuracy on NR3D/SR3D, validating the effectiveness
of prompt-conditioned reconstruction and explicit geometric
reasoning.
Index Terms—streaming 3D reconstruction, language-guided
3D object grounding, neuro-symbolic query graph grounding,
spatial scene graph reasoning.
I. INTRODUCTION
REAL-TIME 3D scene understanding is a cornerstone for
autonomous robots, enabling them to perceive, recon-
struct, reason about, and interact with complex unstructured
environments. Unlike traditional closed-set perception, which
is limited to fixed training categories, open-vocabulary systems
are designed to leverage natural-language category names or
task-specific textual prompts during the inference phrase. This
capability is essential for embodied AI tasks such as mobile
manipulation and semantic navigation [1], [2], where a robot
needs to identify and locate objects from human descriptions.
Despite recent progress in 3D vision-language models,
existing online systems still face a difficult trade-off between
reconstruction efficiency and semantic generalization. Closed-
set online systems such as Embodied-SAM [3] achieve real-
time 3D instance construction, yet they are constrained by
the semantic categories encountered during training. They

To address these challenges, we propose SPEAR-3D, a
framework for prompt-conditioned streaming 3D scene instance
reconstruction and language-guided grounding. As shown

incrementally construct an instance-aware 3D scene from RGB-
D streams. The reconstruction module is designed for streaming
scene construction and achieves 10 FPS on an RTX 3090. We
explicitly distinguish this reconstruction throughput from the
latency of language-query processing. and this design enables
semantic-driven 3D scene instance reconstruction and produces
an explicit instance-aware 3D scene representation, providing
a foundation for subsequent object retrieval, localization, and
language-guided grounding.
Based on the reconstructed 3D instance scene, we introduce a
Neuro-Symbolic Query Graph Grounding (NS-Grounding)
module. Instead of treating the LLM as a black-box spatial
reasoner, we use the LLM to parse natural-language descrip-
tions into structured query graphs. These query graphs are
then explicitly matched to a geometry-derived 3D scene graph
constructed from the output of the streaming reconstruction
process. In this way, language grounding is performed based
on the reconstructed scene state, allowing object localization to
be constrained by explicit 3D geometry and spatial relations.
It is important to clarify the scope of our open-vocabulary set-
ting. SPEAR-3D performs prompt-conditioned open-vocabulary
reconstruction that textual category vocabulary can be supplied
or modified at inference time without the need for retraining.
This differs from unconditional open-world object discovery.
Objects whose names are not included in the inference-time
prompt set may not be reconstructed as semantic instances.
This design choice prioritizes efficient, task-relevant streaming
reconstruction.

II. RELATEDWORK
A. Real-Time 3D Instance Reconstruction
The ability to incrementally reconstruct and parse 3D scenes
is a fundamental prerequisite for embodied agents. Early
semantic SLAM and 3D tracking methods [8], [9] focused
mainly on closed-set categories, limiting a robot’s ability to
operate with novel object names. Subsequent works explored
open-set multimodal scene construction [10], [11], but often
lacked real-time instance-level fusion. Recent online systems
accelerate 3D instance construction, but many of them either
depend on closed-set semantics or introduce high latency
through decoupled processing stages. SPEAR-3D addresses
this trade-off by using prompt-conditioned category guidance
for efficient streaming 3D instance reconstruction.
B. Open-Vocabulary Segmentation and Foundation Models
To overcome closed-set limitations, researchers have in-
tegrated Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and visual foun-
dation models into 3D perception. Offline methods such as
MaskClustering [12] leverage CLIP [13] features to group open-
vocabulary instances, but they are not designed for streaming re-
construction. OnlineAnySeg uses a decoupled pipeline with 2Dinstance segmentation, cross-frame association, and semantic
feature extraction.By incorporating open-vocabulary semantic
features for cross-frame 3D instance fusion, this architecture
enables explicit 3D scene instance reconstruction; however, the
decoupled pipeline constrains reconstruction throughput and
prevents streaming reconstruction..
As shown in Fig. 3, Our method uses SAM3 as a prompt-
conditioned visual segmentation backbone tegrated into a
streaming 3D scene instance reconstruction process. The goal
is not unconditional discovery of every object in an open world,
but efficient reconstruction of task-relevant semantic instances
specified by an inference-time prompt vocabulary. This method
is practical for robotic tasks where object categories can be
provided through task context, user instructions, environment
priors, or benchmark inventories.
C. 3D Visual Grounding and LLM-based Spatial Reasoning
3D visual grounding requires an agent to localize objects
from free-form natural-language descriptions [14], [15]. Many
recent pipelines attempt to inject 3D awareness into LLMs
for spatial reasoning [16], [17]. While LLMs possess robust
language parsing ability, directly employing them as black-box
spatial reasoners may lead to spatial hallucinations. Inspired
by visual programming and neuro-symbolic execution [18],
[19], NS-Grounding separates linguistic parsing from geometric
reasoning. The LLM produces a structured query graph, while
the final spatial decision is made through explicit matching it
against the reconstructed 3D scene graph.
III. METHODOLOGY
SPEAR-3D consists of two connected stages. (1) acategory-
guided 3D streaming reconstructionmodule that incrementally
constructs an instance-aware 3D scene from RGB-D observa-
tions, and (2) aneuro-symbolic query graph groundingmodule
that resolves natural-language queries against the reconstructed
3D scene. In this paper,perceptionis defined as to 2D visual
mask generation and semantic feature extraction, while3D
reconstructionorscene constructionrefers to the incremental
fusion of RGB-D observations into a geometric and instance-
level 3D representation.

As shown in Fig. 2, the reconstruction module constructs a
prompt-conditioned 3D instance scene using streaming RGB-D
frames. Unlike conventional segment-then-classify pipelines,
our method integrates semantic category information prior to
3D instance fusion, reducing per-mask recognition overhead
and enabling efficient streaming updates.

IV. EXPERIMENTS
A. Experimental Settings
We evaluate SPEAR-3D from two complementary perspec-
tives: streaming 3D instance reconstruction and language
grounding over reconstructed scenes. For reconstruction, we
use ScanNet200 and SceneNN, and report AP, AP50, and
AP25. For grounding, we use NR3D and SR3D, and report
Mean Distance (MeanDist) as well as Acc@0.3 and Acc@0.5,
measured against ground-truth target centroids. Following the
prompt-conditioned setting of our method, the inference-time
prompt vocabulary is set to the benchmark category inventory
of each dataset, which makes the evaluation protocol explicit
and reproducible.

B. Hyperparameter Settings.
In the joint graph matching module, the node-relation weight-
ing parameters λuandλecontrol the trade-off between node
matching and relation matching, respectively, with λu+λe= 1.
We sweep λu∈ {0.25,0.50,0.75} on NR3D using Qwen3-plus
to study the influence of this balance. As shown in Table III The
best performance is obtained when λu= 0.50 , which indicates
that semantic consistency and spatial relation reasoning should
contribute equally in our grounding setting.
C. Quantitative Analysis
a) Reconstruction Performance.:As shown in Table I,
SPEAR-3D balances reconstruction accuracy, semantic flexibil-
ity, and computational efficiency. Compared with the decoupled
OnlineAnySeg pipeline, our SAM3-integrated streaming recon-
struction stage achieves10 FPS,while OnlineAnySeg operates
at merely 4 FPS when executing streaming reconstruction for
3D instance scenes. On ScanNet200, SPEAR-3D achieves an
AP25of 60.4%, outperforming OnlineAnySeg by 6.9 percent-
age points. The performance drop on SceneNN is primarily
attributed to the missing annotations of small or ambiguous
objects in the dataset, which conflicts with our category-
driven reconstruction strategy. Unlike ScanNet200, SceneNN’s
incomplete semantic inventory means objects omitted from
the task-relevant prompt vocabulary are skipped during thestreaming stage, thereby impacting strict AP metrics. Ultimately,
this result reflects a calculated trade-off where SPEAR-3D
prioritizes computational efficiency and task-driven precision
over exhaustive scene recall.Additionally, while ESAM exhibits
higher quantitative metrics due to its supervised training
natively optimized on the ScanNet dataset, it operates as a
closed-set validator and lacks the generalized open-vocabulary
capability intrinsic to our framework.”
b) Grounding Performance.:As shown in Table II, NS-
Grounding consistently improves grounding performance across
different LLM backbones. Under the same reconstructed-scene
input, explicit graph matching outperforms direct black-box
LLM reasoning, indicating that structured geometric constraints
help reduce spatial ambiguity and improve target localization.
The best result is achieved by Qwen3-plus with NS-Grounding,
which obtains a MeanDist of 0.96 m and Acc@0.3/Acc@0.5
of 64.7%/65.9% on SR3D, demonstrating the effectiveness of
combining explicit scene-graph reasoning with modern LLMs.
D. Ablation Studies
a) Ablation of NS-Grounding.:Table II isolates the con-
tribution of NS-Grounding by comparing each LLM backbone
with and without the proposed graph-based reasoning module.
Across all backbones, adding NS-Grounding consistently
improves both distance-based and threshold-based grounding
metrics on NR3D and SR3D, indicating that the module is
beneficial regardless of the underlying language model. The
gains are particularly clear for models that rely more heavily
on direct prompting. For instance, Qwen3-plus and GLM-
5 show large improvements after enabling NS-Grounding,
while deepseek-v3.2 also benefits consistently, albeit from
a stronger baseline. On SR3D, all three backbones achieve
substantially better localization accuracy and lower MeanDist
once explicit graph matching is introduced. These results
demonstrate that the proposed module contributes a stable
and model-agnostic improvement by replacing implicit LLM
guessing with structured scene-graph reasoning.
b) Sensitivity to Node-Relation Weighting.:Table III
evaluates the influence of the node-relation weighting parameter
λu. The best performance is achieved at λu= 0.50 , suggesting
that semantic consistency and spatial relation reasoning should
be balanced in our grounding framework. More importantly, the
results remain stable across a relatively wide range of settings.
When λuvaries from 0.25 to 0.75, the performance changes
only moderately, and all configurations produce comparable
grounding accuracy. This indicates that the proposed method is
not sensitive to the exact choice of λu, which reduces the need
for careful hyperparameter tuning and makes the framework
more practical for deployment across different datasets and
LLM backbones.

V. CONCLUSION
We presented SPEAR-3D, a neuro-symbolic framework that
unifies prompt-conditioned streaming 3D instance reconstruc-

tion with explicit language grounding. The main contribution
of this work is to couple category-guided, instance-aware recon-
struction with graph-based reasoning, enabling open-vocabulary
3D scene understanding to proceed in real time while remaining
structurally interpretable. By binding semantic prompts before
3D fusion and then grounding language over reconstructed
scene graphs, SPEAR-3D addresses two key challenges in
existing pipelines: the latency introduced by decoupled online
perception modules and the spatial hallucinations that may arise
when large language models are used as black-box reasoners.
Experiments on ScanNet200, SceneNN, NR3D, and SR3D
demonstrate that SPEAR-3D improves both reconstruction
efficiency and grounding accuracy under the same streaming
reconstructed-scene setting. These results suggest that explicit
geometric reasoning can serve as a reliable bridge between
streaming perception and natural-language grounding, offering
a practical route toward more robust embodied scene under-
standing. More broadly, our framework shows that semantic
flexibility and real-time performance do not have to be mutually
exclusive, and that they can be jointly realized through a
carefully designed reconstruction-and-reasoning pipeline.
A current limitation is that reconstruction recall is still
conditioned on the supplied prompt vocabulary, so objects
outside the prompt set may be missed when the category
inventory is incomplete. In future work, we will investigate hy-
brid category-guided and class-agnostic proposal mechanisms,
together with more efficient query processing and broader open-
world reconstruction, to further improve recall, robustness, and
scalability in streaming 3D perception.
