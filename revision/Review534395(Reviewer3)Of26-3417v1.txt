**Summary**

The paper proposes SPEAR-3D, a two-stage framework that combines:
(1) a streaming 3D semantic instance reconstruction module, reported at
10 FPS on an RTX 3090, which conditions SAM3 segmentation and CLIP
feature binding on an inference-time prompt vocabulary and reuses the
OnlineAnySeg reconstruction backend;
(2) a neuro-symbolic grounding module (NS-Grounding), in which an LLM
parses the natural-language query into a structured query graph that is
matched against a scene graph derived from the reconstructed instances,
using a library of hand-crafted spatial relation scorers.

The direction is reasonable, and the motivation (avoiding black-box LLM
spatial reasoning through explicit geometric matching) is sound.
However, in its current form, the paper has significant issues with the
coherence of the prompt-conditioned evaluation protocol, the omission
of related work and comparisons in the 3D scene graph literature,
insufficient justification and ablation studies for key design choices,
and the absence of any real-robot experiment. 
My assessment is that a major revision is needed.

**Major Comments**

- The paper constructs a geometry-derived 3D scene graph and grounds
language against it, but does not discuss or compare it with the
substantial recent literature on 3D scene graph construction and
language grounding over scene graphs, e.g., [1,2,3,4]. The related work
section needs a dedicated discussion of this line of work, and the
authors should clearly articulate what NS-Grounding does differently
from (or better than) these methods.

-  Integrating semantic category information into the 3D instance
mapping/fusion process is already common practice, e.g., [4,5,6]. The
paper should explain concretely how its "label-mediated feature
binding" differs from these approaches beyond running at a higher frame
rate, ideally with a comparison. The semantic TSDF mapping component
should also be compared with OVI-MAP [7], which addresses a closely
related problem.

- The results on ScanNet are reported in multiple parts of the paper,
including the abstract, but it is not clear what this AP is measuring:
3D instance segmentation? Class-agnostic or not? 

- The pipeline appears to pass the full prompt vocabulary to SAM3 at
every frame (Eq. 2). Prior work [8] handles large vocabularies more
efficiently by first running an open-set recognition model (e.g.,
Recognize Anything Model (RAM)) to propose which categories are present
in the image, and only then, prompting SAM with that reduced set. The
authors should discuss this alternative and, ideally, report how their
per-frame runtime scales with vocabulary size; the 10 FPS claim is
presumably not independent of the number of prompts.

- Sec. III-B.4 introduces seven hand-crafted scorers for relations, but
the paper provides no justification for why this particular pool of
relations is sufficient, nor an ablation showing the contribution of
each scorer.

-All experiments are conducted offline on ScanNet200/SceneNN/NR3D/SR3D
sequences. There is no demonstration on a physical robot, no evaluation
under real sensor noise, motion blur, and no closed-loop task using the
grounding output. 

- Several symbols are used without definition, e.g., the operator
ID(l_k) over the arrow in Eq. (3).

**Minor Comment**

The text contains many typos and grammatical errors, for example:

- Abstract: "RGB-D streams,and" (missing space)
- Sec. I: "inference phrase" -> "inference phase"
- Sec. III-B.4a: "Directional Relations.:" (stray period before the
colon; this pattern recurs in the other paragraph headings)
- Sec. IV-C.a: "recall.Additionally" (missing space)

This list is not exhaustive. High-quality work should also pay
attention to these details, which are easily checkable with today's
tools. As a practical tip, proofreading the paper from the bottom up
makes such errors easier to spot if the authors prefer not to automate
the check.
