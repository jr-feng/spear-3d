This paper presents SPEAR-3D, a pipeline combining prompt-conditioned
streaming 3D instance mapping with an LLM-parsed query graph and
explicit geometric relation scoring. The problem is relevant to RA-L,
and externalizing spatial reasoning into an inspectable geometric
procedure is a reasonable design choice. The manuscript also
appropriately distinguishes reconstruction throughput from query-time
latency and acknowledges that reconstruction recall is conditioned on
the supplied prompt vocabulary. The visualization, the design of the
figures are also clear and amazing.

However, the current manuscript does not yet provide sufficient
methodological consistency, experimental rigor, or differentiation from
prior work to substantiate its main claims.

First, according to the paper, some of the reported grounding results
appear internally inconsistent.
In Table II, the GLM-5 baseline on SR3D has Acc@0.3 = 52.5 but Acc@0.5
= 51.9. Since every prediction within 0.3 m is also within 0.5 m, this
result should not be possible under the stated metric. The claims about
the “best” model also do not fully match the table. Please recheck the
evaluation code and all reported numbers, because this currently raises
concerns about the reliability of the experimental results.

Second, the mathematical formulation does not match the proposed
“between” relation. The paper defines the query structure as a normal
graph with binary edges, but the between relation depends jointly on
one target and two reference objects. In other words, it is a ternary
constraint, not two independent binary relations as shown in Fig. 5.
The authors should revise the formulation using a higher-order factor
or hyperedge and make the figure, equations, and implementation
consistent.

Another problem is that the grounding evaluation is not sufficient to
support the main claims. The paper uses custom centroid-distance
metrics instead of the standard target-instance accuracy commonly used
for NR3D and SR3D. A nearby wrong object may therefore still be counted
as correct, and the results cannot be directly compared with prior
grounding methods. Please report the standard benchmark metrics,
clearly explain any query filtering, and compare against existing
structured or constraint-based 3D grounding approaches rather than only
against direct LLM prompting.

So generally speaking, I think the problems on the paper is more than
just edit, but should really revise and resubmit.
